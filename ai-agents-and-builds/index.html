Keep your Mac awake for AI agents, builds and downloads

Long jobs fail or pause when your Mac goes to sleep. Here's how to keep it running, and still let the screen lock.

Wake while your tools are open

In Hootle's Settings, under Wake Up for Apps, add the app your work runs in: Terminal, iTerm2, Ghostty, Warp, VS Code, Cursor, the Claude or ChatGPT app, Xcode, Final Cut Pro or Logic. Hootle stays awake while any of them is open and goes back to sleep when they all quit.

To be clear about what this does: Hootle watches whether the app is open, not whether an agent or build inside it is busy. Quit the app, or click the owl, when you're done.

Let the screen lock, keep the Mac working

Untick Keep Screen On in Hootle's menu. Your Mac keeps running the job, but the display can dim, start the screen saver and lock like normal, which is safer if you walk away and kinder to your battery.

Know when it's done

Set a timer, or turn on Notify When On… to get a reminder every few hours that Hootle is still keeping your Mac awake. On battery power Hootle goes to sleep at 10% (you can change the level), so a forgotten job won't drain your MacBook.

What about the lid?

A MacBook still sleeps when you close the lid, unless it's on power with an external display, keyboard or mouse. Apps from the Mac App Store can't change that.

Prefer the command line?

caffeinate -i -w <pid> keeps the Mac awake until a specific process ends. See How to keep your Mac awake.
