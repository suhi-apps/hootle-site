How to keep your Mac awake

Three ways to stop your Mac from sleeping, dimming its screen or locking, from built-in settings to a one-click app.

1. Change your Mac's settings

In System Settings, open Lock Screen and set "Turn display off when inactive" to a longer time or Never. On a MacBook, Battery › Options also has "Prevent automatic sleeping on power adapter when the display is off". The catch: these change your Mac for good, so you have to remember to change them back, or your battery and screen pay for it.

2. Use the caffeinate command

macOS has a built-in Terminal command. It keeps the Mac awake until you press ⌃C or close the window:

caffeinate -d          # keep the display (and Mac) awake
caffeinate -i          # keep the Mac awake, let the display sleep
caffeinate -d -t 3600  # stay awake for one hour
caffeinate -w 1234     # stay awake until process 1234 finishes

It's free and precise, but you need a Terminal window open and nothing tells you at a glance that it's running.

3. Use Hootle

Hootle is a free menu bar app: a little owl that keeps your Mac awake while its eyes are open. Click it to turn it on, click again to turn it off, or set a timer from 15 minutes to 4 hours, any length, or until a time of day. It can also turn itself on while apps like Zoom, Keynote or a terminal are open, and it knows when to stop: it goes to sleep when the timer ends, when the battery gets low, or when you've walked away.

Can I keep a MacBook awake with the lid closed?

Closing the lid puts a MacBook to sleep unless it's connected to power and an external display, keyboard or mouse (clamshell mode). Apps from the Mac App Store, Hootle included, can't override that.
